Adding files

  • Folders include their subfolders. Folder and Ctrl+Shift+O take the files in every folder inside, up to 1,000, and say so if there are more.
  • Paste a picture. Copy a screenshot or a picture from a web page, then press Ctrl+V in Upscaly to add it.
  • Damaged pictures are caught. A broken or half-saved picture is marked Can’t be used as soon as you add it, instead of failing later.

The queue

  • A queue down the side. Every file has a small picture on the left with its own progress line; rest the pointer on it for the full list.
  • Clear Pause and Stop. Pause holds a video after the part it is on and carries on from there; Stop asks first, then ends the whole queue.

Choosing what to run

  • Works without internet. With no connection, the built-in models still work, and Models says so instead of waiting.
  • The tab follows your file. Add a video and the Video tab comes up by itself; add a photo and you are back on Photo.
  • Models named for what they do. Everyday Photos, Old & Blurry Photos, Keep Film Grain, Fine Detail, Maximum Detail, Illustrations and the Animation Video models.
  • Everyday Photos to start. Every tab starts on Everyday Photos where it fits, and your last pick is remembered.
  • Sizes the model can really make. Scale buttons stop at what the chosen model can do, and a greyed-out button says why.
  • Files run in the order shown. Pressing Upscale while something runs adds the work to the end of the queue.

Settings

  • Told when there is no graphics card. If no graphics card can be used, Upscaly says once, plainly, that it is using the slower processor.
  • Crash reports cover more. With Send crash reports on in More settings, errors on screen are reported too, still with names and folders removed.
  • Your graphics card, in plain sight. The chip at the top shows the card doing the work, and the app no longer slips onto the slower processor without telling you.
  • Output size, explained. Each way of setting the size says what it does, and the menu no longer flickers.
  • Enhancement amount. The slider rests at 50% on the way, and its labels jump straight to their value.
  • Denoise for every model. Denoise always shows; where it does not apply it is greyed out with the reason.
  • Restore faces, explained. Each control says what it does, you pick which faces to restore and how strongly, and the face model downloads only after you agree to its licence.
  • Protect parts of a picture. Paint over text or a logo to keep it untouched. Move, or holding Space, lets you look around; Undo, Redo and Clear cover every brush.
  • Better recipes. Two new ready-made recipes, copies named "... copy", and saving the same settings twice tells you which recipe has them.
  • A shorter Settings panel. The things you change most are in Settings; the rest moved to More settings, which now replaces the panel instead of covering it.
  • Notify when the queue is done. Choose Notify under When batch finishes and Windows shows a real message at the end.
  • A steadier widget. It comes back when switched on again, no longer shows "--:--", and hides when nothing is running.

Results

  • Report a result. If a result looks wrong, Report a result opens an email to us with the app version and model filled in.
  • Done, with the new size. After a photo, a row says how long it took and how big the result is, with Split, Side and zoom beside it.
  • Copy and Open. Copy puts the result on the clipboard (Ctrl+Shift+C); Open shows it in its folder.
  • Compare two models. Press R on a photo to try two models on one part of it, side by side, before running the whole thing.

Video

  • Frame steps land exactly. Stepping one frame now moves the original and the result to that exact frame.
  • Video tools set up by Upscaly. The video tools now download inside Upscaly itself the first time you upscale a video.
  • A new video bar. Play, step frame by frame and see the time as you move along the line.
  • Start here and End here. Mark the part of the video you want upscaled; the keys I and O do the same.
  • Render sample. Upscale a few seconds first to judge the result before running the whole video.

Questions.

Does Upscaly upload my photos?+

No. The work happens on your own graphics card and your files never leave your computer. Upscaly only goes online to fetch the models and video tool you choose.

How much does it cost?+

On Windows you get 15 days free with the full app and nothing watermarked, then it is $9.99 once. No subscription and no credits. On Android it is free.

What do I need to run it on Windows?+

Windows 10 or 11 and 8 GB of memory. A graphics card that supports Vulkan is recommended.

Does it work without internet?+

Yes. The built-in models always work offline.

Can it upscale video?+

Yes. Upscaly turns video into HD or 4K. You can step through it frame by frame, render a short sample first, and the sound is kept.

Can I use the results for commercial work?+

Yes. You may use Upscaly for personal or commercial work, including images you plan to publish or sell. Your files stay yours.
