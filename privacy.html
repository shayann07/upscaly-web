Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 November 2026

Upscaly is a photo and video upscaling tool that runs entirely on your own device. This policy explains, in plain terms, what that means for your data. It covers both Upscaly for Windows and Upscaly for Android.

What Upscaly does with your files

Every image or video you upscale is processed locally, on your own CPU and GPU. It is never uploaded, transmitted, or sent to us or to any third party — not for processing, not for backup, not for any other reason. There is no server involved in upscaling at all.

Your original files are never modified. Upscaly writes each result as a new file and leaves the source exactly as it was.

What we collect

Never your photos, videos or file names. Upscaly has no user accounts and no login. On Windows, nothing is collected unless you turn on crash reports. On Android, crash reports and anonymous usage data are on by default and can be switched off in Settings (details in the Android section below). Either way we cannot see what you upscale, and we do not know who you are.

Crash reports (Windows, off by default). If you turn on Send crash reports in Settings and Upscaly crashes, the next launch sends one report to Sentry, our crash-reporting provider, which stores it in the EU. A report holds the app version, the Windows version, the error and where in Upscaly's code it happened, and, for the upscaling engine, the graphics card name, the model and the image size. Folder paths, file names, your user name and your pictures are removed before sending. IP addresses are not stored, though Sentry may derive an approximate country or city from the connection. Turn the setting off at any time to stop sending.

Why we use crash reports (Windows). Only to find and fix crashes in Upscaly. They are never used for advertising, never sold, and never combined with other data.

How they are kept safe (Windows). Reports are sent over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection and stored by Sentry in the EU. Only the Upscaly developer can read them. Sentry deletes each report automatically after at most 90 days.

Your choices and rights (Windows). Crash reports are sent only with your consent, which you give by turning the setting on and can withdraw at any time by turning it off. If you live in the EU, UK or a similar jurisdiction, you have the right to ask what we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to complain to your data-protection authority. Because reports carry no name, account or IP address, we usually cannot tell which reports are yours; email us the approximate date and time of the crash and we will find and delete the matching reports within 30 days.

Upscaly does need access to the photos and videos you choose to open, in order to upscale them. That access happens only on your device, only for the files you select, and the files never leave it.

Crash reports and usage data (Android)

From version 1.0.5, Upscaly for Android uses two Google Firebase services, with Google acting as our service provider: Firebase Crashlytics for crash reports and Google Analytics for Firebase for anonymous usage events. Both are on by default. You can turn both off at any time in the app under Settings → Send crash reports and usage data. Your photos are never part of either.

Crash reports (Crashlytics) contain: the error and where in the code it happened (stack trace), the app version, your device model, the Android version, free memory and disk space, the graphics (GPU/Vulkan) level and chip name, the AI model in use, and a random Crashlytics installation ID. If an upscale or a model install fails without crashing, a short error message is sent as well, with file paths removed.

Usage events (Analytics) are: upscale started and finished (model, scale, a rough duration range and whether it succeeded), result saved, a promo card shown or used, the review prompt requested, and a bundled model failing to install. Firebase also records its automatic events (first open, app updates, session start), your device model, Android version, app version, an approximate country that Google derives from your IP address (Google does not store the IP address), and a random app-instance ID. They contain no photos, file names, names, email addresses or precise location.

Why. Only to find and fix crashes and to see which features and models are used, so we can improve Upscaly. The data is not used for advertising, not sold, and not combined with other data. Upscaly for Android does not read your advertising ID.

How long it is kept. Google keeps Crashlytics crash data for 90 days before it starts removing it from its live and backup systems. Analytics event data is kept for 2 months. Data is sent over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection, and only the Upscaly developer can read it.

Your choices. Switch the setting off and nothing further is sent. Uninstalling the app resets the random IDs. Because the IDs are random and carry no name or account, we usually cannot tell which data is yours; to ask what we hold or to have it deleted, email us the app version and the approximate date and we will help within 30 days. Google's handling of this data is described in the Firebase privacy information at firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

Purchases

Upscaly for Windows is bought through the Microsoft Store. Upscaly for Android is free on Google Play and has no in-app purchases. Payment, trials and licensing are handled entirely by Microsoft or Google under their own privacy statements. We never see your payment details. The stores give us aggregated sales reports; they do not identify you to us.

Network activity

Upscaly makes network requests for these purposes only:

  • Downloading AI models you choose to install. Model files are fetched from public repositories on GitHub. These are one-directional downloads — no image, file name, or personal information is included in the request.
  • Checking the list of available models. Upscaly reads a public list of models from this website (upscaly.shayxo.dev) so it can show you what is available. No image, file name, or personal information is included in the request.
  • Downloading FFmpeg (Windows only), the video toolkit used to read and write video files. It is fetched from its public upstream release on GitHub the first time you upscale a video. No image, file name, or personal information is included in the request.
  • Sending crash reports (Windows, only if you turn them on), as described above, to Sentry.
  • Sending crash reports and usage events (Android, on by default, off in Settings), as described above, to Google Firebase.

App updates on Windows are delivered by the Microsoft Store, not by Upscaly itself.

As with any web request, the servers involved (GitHub's, which also host this website) may log standard technical information such as your IP address, in line with their own privacy policies. We do not operate those servers and do not receive that information.

Permissions

On Android, Upscaly requests only what it needs to function. It does not request the advertising ID permission:

  • Photos — Upscaly uses Android's system photo picker, which lets you choose individual photos without granting the app access to your whole photo library.
  • Notifications — used to show upscaling progress and to tell you when a job has finished.
  • Storage (older Android versions only) — used to save results to your gallery. Not required on modern Android versions.
  • Internet — used for the model downloads and for sending the optional crash reports and usage data described above.

Where your results are stored

Upscaled files are saved to your own device — your gallery or chosen folder. They are not backed up, synced, or mirrored anywhere by Upscaly. Deleting them is entirely up to you, and once deleted they are gone; we hold no copy because we never had one.

Third-party models

The AI models Upscaly can download are produced and published by third parties, and are distributed under their own respective licenses. Downloading a model is a request to a public file host; it does not create an account or a relationship with the model's author.

Children's privacy

Upscaly is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age. Apart from the optional crash reports and anonymous usage data described above, which contain no names, accounts or photos, it collects no information at all.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new "last updated" date. If a change affects what is collected, the app will tell you, and on Windows it will ask before sending anything new.

Contact
